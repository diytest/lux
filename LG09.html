Graphical luxmeter LG09 USB


Description of the luxmeter: descriptionLG09.pdf

Features of the luxmeter LG09 USB
- ability to record and reproduce as a graph periodic light pulse sequences and single light pulses, which allows measuring the parameters and shape of signals from such light sources as  rescue beacons, signal flares, pyrotechnic products;

- ability to perform 490 measurements once a second (or less frequently), calculate the average value from their results and plot it on a graph, which allows observing slowly changing light processes, for example, evaluating the change in workplace illumination during the day. Such measurements can be accompanied by plotting the temperature (luxmeter LG09T USB)
- if necessary, the luxmeter LG09 USB can be supplemented with a function of issuing a control signal for switching on/off depending on the level of illumination, temperature (luxmeter LG09T USB -  this will make it easy to organize thermostating).

8 ranges, a recording duration of up to 5s at a sampling frequency of 8.25kHz, the ability to calculate the average value of light signals and plot the calculated values once a second or less frequently, as well as the ability to measure and plot temperature provide the USB luxmeter LG09 with a wide range of applications.
is designed on a photodiode with high sensitivity, the light-sensitive surface is 10mm in diameter, 

Parameters of the luxmeter LG09 USB
-special light filters are used, the spectral sensitivity of the photodiode-light filter pair of the luxmeter matches the spectral sensitivity of the human eye,
-8 measurement ranges of the luxmeter, the scale of the most sensitive: 0..0.5lx (0..5000x10-4lx), the least sensitive 0..5000000lx (0..5000x10+3lx),
-automatic selection of the luxmeter measurement ranges (x10-4, x10-3, ..., x10+3lx),
-possibility of manual selection of the luxmeter measurement limits,
-the signal sampling frequency by the luxmeter analog-to-digital converter is 8.25kHz,
-the number of samples performed by the luxmeter during one measurement and displayed as a graph on the computer display is up to 32767 samples
-display of MINIMUM, MAXIMUM, AVERAGE value, and the signal RIPPLE FACTOR
-possibility to increase the duration of a single measurement by the luxmeter by programmatically decreasing the sampling frequency by 2...255 times

  With the transition to LED lighting, measuring the ripple of light sources has become particularly relevant.
While the ripple of incandescent and energy-saving lamps met the requirements of SNiP and differed little among lamps from different manufacturers, LED lamps, including those "from Europe", often turn out to be unsuitable for lighting workplaces and rest areas due to their ripple level.
And using lamps with a high level of ripple to illuminate rotating objects is generally hazardous - due to the stroboscopic effect, for example, a rotating machine spindle may appear stationary.

The illumination ripple factor is calculated by the formula: Kp = (Emax. - Emin.) / (2 x Eavg.),
where Emax, Emin., Eavg. are the maximum, minimum, and average values of illumination.



Incandescent lamps have thermal inertia; when the supplying AC voltage passes through zero, the filament does not have enough time to cool down significantly, so the lamp brightness drops only slightly.
Kp=0.17, ripple frequency Fp=100Hz (this is double the 50Hz 220V mains frequency - the supplying AC voltage reaches zero twice per period)







For the energy-saving lamp Philips 14W, Kp=0.067, Fp=100Hz






LED lamps have low inertia. If the manufacturer saved on the lamp driver components, the same 100 Hz ripple turns out to be huge - when the supplying AC voltage passes through zero, the current through the LEDs becomes zero and the LEDs manage to turn off.
